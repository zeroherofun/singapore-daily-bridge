US and China hold working-level talks on shipping and tariffs

By Staff Reporter 4 min read

Officials from the United States and China met in Geneva this week for working-level discussions on shipping fees, port access, and retaliatory tariffs that have affected trans-Pacific trade routes over the past year.

Both sides described the two-day session as frank but constructive. Negotiators reviewed disputed surcharges imposed on container lines and agreed to share updated data on vessel wait times at major entry ports.

No formal agreement was announced, though participants said they would reconvene next month to assess whether interim measures could reduce backlogs affecting electronics and automotive supply chains.

Shipping analysts in Singapore noted that any easing of restrictions could benefit regional transshipment hubs, but cautioned that broader geopolitical tensions may limit the scope of concessions. Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore said it is tracking developments that could affect cargo volumes through the Strait of Malacca.

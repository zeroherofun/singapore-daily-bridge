Japan pledges aid after earthquake hits northern coastal towns

By Staff Reporter 4 min read

A magnitude 6.8 earthquake struck off Japan's northern coast on Thursday, damaging homes, disrupting rail services, and prompting temporary tsunami advisories for several prefectures facing the Pacific Ocean.

Japanese authorities said at least 42 people were injured, mostly from falling objects and broken glass. Emergency crews searched collapsed structures in two fishing towns where older wooden buildings bore the brunt of the shaking.

Prime Minister Fumio Kishida directed disaster response agencies to deploy medical teams, portable shelters, and water supplies to affected areas. Power was restored to most districts within 12 hours, though some rural roads remained blocked by landslides.

Singapore's Ministry of Foreign Affairs said there were no reports of Singaporeans seriously hurt. The Singapore Red Cross said it is monitoring requests for assistance from Japanese relief organisations and may channel donations through established humanitarian partners.

EU ministers agree on framework for cross-border energy grid upgrades

By Staff Reporter 4 min read

European Union energy ministers reached a provisional agreement in Brussels on a framework to coordinate cross-border electricity grid upgrades and share costs among member states.

The plan sets targets for interconnecting national grids by 2030, with priority corridors identified in central and eastern Europe where bottlenecks have limited the flow of renewable power during peak production periods.

Under the framework, countries will submit joint project proposals for high-voltage lines and battery storage facilities. A central fund, financed through existing climate levies, would cover up to 40 per cent of eligible construction costs.

Officials said the agreement still requires approval from the European Parliament. Energy analysts noted that clearer grid rules could help stabilise wholesale prices, though individual member states retain discretion over domestic tariff structures and subsidy schemes.
